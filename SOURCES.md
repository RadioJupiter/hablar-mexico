# Sources

Expressions taught as Mexican usage were checked against the sources below. If a colorful word or phrase does not appear in these works in the sense used here, it is not taught. English explanations are this course's, not quotations from the dictionaries.

## Dictionaries

- Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española, [*Diccionario de americanismos*](https://www.asale.org/damer/) (DAMER). Used for *¿qué onda?* (Mexico, popular: to ask what is going on, and to greet), *camión* (Mexico: a passenger bus), *¡órale!* (Mexico and nearby countries: agreement, a push to do something, or surprise), and *¡ándale!* (Mexico: urging someone to act, agreement, surprise, or a push to act in a certain way).
- El Colegio de México, [*Diccionario del español de México*](https://dem.colmex.mx/) (DEM). Used for *¡mande!* (a courtesy reply when someone calls you, or when you have not heard something), *¿a cómo?* (what is the price; “¿A cómo el kilo de jitomates?”), *¿cómo estás? / ¿cómo está?* as a greeting, and *(buen) provecho* (a courtesy wish that someone enjoy what they are eating).
- Real Academia Española and ASALE, [*Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*](https://www.rae.es/dpd/ahorita), entry *ahorita*. Records *ahorita* as the diminutive of *ahora*, common in colloquial speech across much of the Americas, and notes that in Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic *ahora* and *ahorita* are distinguished differently than they often are elsewhere.
- Academia Mexicana de la Lengua, *Diccionario breve de mexicanismos* of Guido Gómez de Silva, entry *salir*: *¿sale?* is recorded as “¿De acuerdo?”

## Other notes used with care

- Academia Mexicana de la Lengua, consultation on *¿qué onda?*, and Concepción Company's remarks on *¿mande?* as Mexican courtesy (discussed in AML and in reporting that quotes her). These are background, not the sole warrant for teaching the phrases.
- Prices, street names, and taco orders in the units are invented practice material. They are not current market prices or real businesses.

## Method

The teaching sequence (explain, model, practice in a real situation, then check what was just taught) follows ordinary practice in communicative language teaching, with a first emphasis on comprehensible input and high-frequency chunks rather than isolated word lists or sentence translation.
