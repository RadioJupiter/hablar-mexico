# Unit 9 · When something goes wrong

Sooner or later you will not understand, you will lose something, or you will feel unwell. This unit gives you calm, simple lines for getting help, so a problem does not leave you without words.

## When something goes wrong {#main}

<p class="meta">About 45 minutes · Situation: not understanding, asking for help, and explaining a problem</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

**When you do not understand.** *¿Mande?* (Unit 1) asks someone to repeat. When repeating is not enough, use these. *No entiendo* is “I don't understand.” *¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor?* is “Could you speak more slowly, please?” (*usted*). *¿Qué quiere decir…?* is “What does … mean?”: *¿Qué quiere decir “camión”?* *¿Cómo se dice … en español?* is “How do you say … in Spanish?” Saying *Estoy aprendiendo español* (I'm learning Spanish) usually makes people slow down and help.

**Asking for help.** *¿Me puede ayudar?* is “Can you help me?” to someone you address as *usted*, which is right with a stranger. Start with *Disculpe* (Unit 4) to get their attention: *Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar?*

**Explaining a problem.** Simple past forms of a few verbs go a long way. *Perdí mi celular* is “I lost my phone.” *Perdí mi cartera* is “I lost my wallet.” *Me robaron la cartera* is “My wallet was stolen” (literally, “they stole the wallet from me”). *Me equivoqué de camión* is “I took the wrong bus.” *Estoy perdido* (a man) or *Estoy perdida* (a woman) is “I'm lost.” Learn these as whole lines; the past tense itself comes in a later unit.

**Feeling unwell.** *Me siento mal* is “I feel sick.” Pain uses *doler*, which works like *gustar* in Unit 8: the thing that hurts is the subject. *Me duele la cabeza* (my head hurts), *Me duele el estómago* (my stomach hurts), *Me duelen los pies* (my feet hurt, plural). Spanish uses *la* or *el* here, not “my.” *Necesito un médico* or *Necesito un doctor* is “I need a doctor.”

**Emergencies.** *¡Ayuda!* is “Help!” *Llame a una ambulancia* is “Call an ambulance” (*usted*). *Es una emergencia* is “It's an emergency.” Mexico's national emergency number is 911.

The situations in the conversation are invented practice. This unit teaches language, not medical or legal advice.

</div>

<h3>Model conversation</h3>

<p class="scene no-speak">A visitor who has lost a phone asks a stranger, then a police officer, for help. Later, at a hotel desk, the visitor feels unwell. Everything is invented.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar? Perdí mi celular.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me, can you help me? I lost my phone.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Woman:</span> <span lang="es">Ay, qué mal. Pregúntele al policía de la esquina.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Oh, that's bad. Ask the police officer on the corner.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Perdón, no entiendo. ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? Estoy aprendiendo español.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Sorry, I don't understand. Could you speak more slowly, please? I'm learning Spanish.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Woman:</span> <span lang="es">Claro. Ahí, en la esquina, hay un policía.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Of course. There, on the corner, there's a police officer.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Muchas gracias.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Thank you very much.</span></p>
<p class="scene no-speak">Later, at the hotel desk.</p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Buenas noches. Me siento mal. Me duele mucho el estómago.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Good evening. I feel sick. My stomach hurts a lot.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Clerk:</span> <span lang="es">¿Necesita un médico?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Do you need a doctor?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, por favor. ¿Me puede ayudar a llamar?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, please. Can you help me call?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Clerk:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, cómo no. Ahorita le llamo.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, of course. I'll call for you right away.</span></p>

<div class="note" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Entiendo* is en-TYEN-doh. *Despacio* is des-PAH-syoh; the *ci* is an *s* sound in Mexico. *Ayudar* is ah-yoo-DAR, with a tapped *r* at the end. *Perdí* is per-DEE, stress on the last syllable because of the accent; *perdido* is per-DEE-doh. *Duele* is DWEH-leh, two syllables. *Emergencia* is eh-mer-HEN-syah: the *g* before *e* is the breathy *h*. *Ambulancia* is ahm-boo-LAHN-syah.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Say *no entiendo* and *¿puede hablar más despacio?* early. Get help with *disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar?* Explain with whole lines: *perdí mi…*, *me robaron…*, *estoy perdido / perdida*. Pain: *me duele* + one thing, *me duelen* + several. Emergencies: *¡ayuda!*, *llame a una ambulancia*; the number in Mexico is 911.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me · shadowing</h3>

<p>Play the model and speak the visitor's lines. Say *no entiendo* and *más despacio* calmly and clearly; these are the lines you most need when you are flustered. Pay attention to *me duele mucho el estómago*: *mucho* goes after the verb.</p>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor?*
2. *¿Qué quiere decir…?*; *¿Cómo se dice … en español?*
3. *Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar?*
4. *Perdí mi celular.* *Me robaron la cartera.*
5. The thing that hurts is the subject. *La cabeza* is one thing, so *duele*; *los pies* are several, so *duelen*.
6. 911. *Llame a una ambulancia.*

**Practice.**
1. A model: *Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar? Me equivoqué de camión. Estoy perdido* (or *perdida*).
2. A model: *Me duele el estómago. Me duelen los pies. Me duele mucho la cabeza.*
