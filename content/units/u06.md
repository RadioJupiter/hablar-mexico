# Unit 6 · Invitations and being a guest

This unit puts the earlier pieces to work in someone's home: suggesting a time, walking in, and sitting down to eat. It also settles two words you will hear constantly: *¿sale?* and *ahorita*.

## Invitations and being a guest {#main}

<p class="meta">About 45 minutes · Situation: making a plan, arriving, and eating as a guest</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

An invitation in Mexico is often warm and direct. You need a way to suggest a time, a way to agree, and the courtesy lines for walking into a house and sitting down to a meal.

**Making a plan.** *¿Te gustaría venir el sábado?* is “Would you like to come on Saturday?” (*tú*). With *usted* it is *¿Le gustaría venir el sábado?* *¿Te queda bien a las siete?* is “Does seven work for you?” Clock time uses *a las* + the hour: *a las siete*, *a las siete y media* (7:30), *a las ocho en punto* (8:00 sharp), *como a las siete* (around seven). Morning hours can add *de la mañana*; evening hours *de la noche*; 1:00 uses *a la una*.

**Agreeing: *¿Sale?*** In Mexican speech, *¿Sale?* is a common way to ask “Agreed? Does that work?” The *Diccionario breve de mexicanismos* of the Academia Mexicana de la Lengua records *¿sale?* as *¿De acuerdo?* A natural answer is *Sale.* or *Sale, ahí nos vemos.* It is informal. With someone you address as *usted*, prefer *¿Le parece bien?* or *¿Le queda bien?* and *Muy bien, ahí nos vemos.*

**Coming in.** *Pásale* is the everyday informal invitation to come in: *pasar* plus the Mexican *le* that softens the line. *Pase usted* is the formal version. *Siéntate* is “sit down” (*tú*); *siéntese* is *usted*. As you step in, *con permiso* is the right courtesy, which you already used in Unit 5.

***Órale* and *ándale*.** Both are informal, and both have more than one use, so learn them as reactions, not as a single English word. The *Diccionario de americanismos* records *¡órale!* in Mexico as agreement, as a push to do something, and as surprise. *¡Ándale!* is recorded as urging someone to act, as agreement, as surprise, and as a push to act in a certain way. In a doorway you may hear *Ándale, pásale* (“go on, come in”). After you agree on a plan, *Órale* can simply mean “all right.” Do not use either with someone you are addressing formally until you can hear how they talk.

**At the table.** *¿Gustas un café?* is “Would you like a coffee?” (*tú*); *¿Gusta usted un café?* with *usted*. *(Buen) provecho* is said to people who are eating: the *Diccionario del español de México* describes it as a courtesy wish that someone enjoy the food. You can say it as you sit down, as you pass a table, or as you leave one. While you eat, *Está rico* or *Está bien rico, gracias* is enough praise. When you get up, *Con permiso* again, then *Muchas gracias por la comida* or *Gracias por invitarme*.

***Ahorita*, with its real range.** The *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas* records *ahorita* as the diminutive of *ahora*, common in colloquial speech across much of the Americas. In Mexican conversation the word is flexible. *Ahorita vengo* is “I'll be right back,” which may be very soon. *Ahorita se los paso* (Unit 2) and *Ahorita pasa el camión* (Unit 4) mean “in a moment” or “shortly.” *Ahorita no puedo* is “I can't just now.” Do not hear *ahorita* as English “right this second” every time. Tone and context tell you whether it is immediate or looser. If you need to be precise yourself, use *enseguida* or *ahora mismo* for “right away,” and *en un rato* for “in a little while.”

The time in the conversation is an invented practice time, not a real appointment.

<h3>Model conversation</h3>

<p class="scene no-speak">Elena invites a friend over. Later that Saturday, the friend arrives and is asked to sit down to eat. The time is invented.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">¿Te gustaría venir el sábado a comer? Como a las dos.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Would you like to come on Saturday to eat? Around two.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Friend:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, me encantaría. ¿A las dos en punto?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, I'd love to. Two on the dot?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">Como a las dos, no hay prisa. ¿Te queda bien? ¿Sale?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Around two, there's no rush. Does that work for you? Agreed?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Friend:</span> <span lang="es">Sale, ahí nos vemos.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Agreed, see you then.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">Órale. Ahí te espero.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">All right. I'll be expecting you.</span></p>
<p class="scene no-speak">Saturday, at the door.</p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">¡Pásale! Qué bueno que viniste. Ándale, siéntate.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Come in! How good that you came. Go on, sit down.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Friend:</span> <span lang="es">Con permiso. Gracias por invitarme.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me (coming in). Thanks for inviting me.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">Provecho. ¿Gustas un agua?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Enjoy your meal. Would you like a water?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Friend:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, por favor. Está bien rico. Gracias.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, please. This is really good. Thank you.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">Ahorita te sirvo más.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">I'll serve you some more in a moment.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Friend:</span> <span lang="es">Con permiso. Muchas gracias por la comida.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me (getting up). Thank you very much for the meal.</span></p>

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Gustaría* is goos-tah-REE-ah, four clear vowels. *Sábado* is SAH-bah-doh, stress on the first syllable because of the accent. *Encantaría* is en-kahn-tah-REE-ah. *Sale* is SAH-leh, two syllables, not English “sale.” *Órale* is OH-rah-leh; *ándale* is AHN-dah-leh. *Pásale* is PAH-sah-leh, stress on the first syllable because of the accent. *Provecho* is proh-VEH-choh. *Comida* is koh-MEE-dah. When you say *ahorita*, do not swallow the first *a*: ah-oh-REE-tah, four syllables, stress on *ri*.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Suggest a time with *a las…* and check it with *¿Te queda bien?* Informal agreement is *¿Sale?* / *Sale.* At the door: *pásale* (or *pase usted*), and you say *con permiso*. At the table: *provecho*, *está rico*, and *gracias por la comida*. *Ahorita* can mean right now, in a moment, or a looser soon; read it from context.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me · shadowing</h3>

<p>Play the first half of the model and listen for the plan being settled: *como a las dos*, then *¿sale?*, then *sale, ahí nos vemos.* Play the door and table half and speak the friend's lines: *con permiso*, *gracias por invitarme*, *está bien rico*, and the closing thanks. Keep *sale* and *ahorita* in Spanish; do not swap in English “okay” and “right now” as you shadow.</p>

### Practice

1. Invite a friend (tú) to eat on Sunday around two, check that the time works, and close with *¿sale?*
2. Write the formal versions of three informal lines: *¿Te gustaría venir?*, *¿Sale?*, and *¡Pásale!*

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *A las siete*; *a las siete y media*; *como a las siete.*
2. It asks “Agreed? Does that work?” It is informal.
3. *Con permiso.* You can add *gracias* or *gracias por invitarme.*
4. *Provecho* or *buen provecho.*
5. It can mean “right now / in a moment” or a looser “soon,” depending on context. For more precision: *enseguida* or *ahora mismo* (right away), and *en un rato* (in a little while).
6. *Con permiso. Muchas gracias por la comida.* (*Gracias por invitarme* is also good as you leave.)

**Practice.**
1. A model: *¿Te gustaría venir el domingo a comer, como a las dos? ¿Te queda bien? ¿Sale?*
2. *¿Le gustaría venir?*; *¿Le parece bien?* or *¿Le queda bien?*; *Pase usted.*
