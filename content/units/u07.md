# Unit 7 · The corner store and the pharmacy

Most small errands in Mexico happen at a counter: a corner store, a pharmacy, a stationery shop. This unit gives you the lines for asking whether they have something, asking for it, and paying, plus the few pharmacy questions a traveler actually needs.

## The corner store and the pharmacy {#main}

<p class="meta">About 40 minutes · Situation: asking for things at a counter and paying</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

In many small shops the goods are behind the counter, so you ask for what you want instead of picking it up yourself. Greet first, as in Unit 1: *Buenas tardes.* Then ask.

**Do you have it?** *¿Tiene…?* is “Do you have…?” said to one person as *usted*, which is the safe choice with a shopkeeper. *¿Tienen…?* asks about the shop as a whole: “Do you (all) have…?” *¿Tiene pilas?* “Do you have batteries?” *¿Tienen agua mineral?* The answers you will hear are *Sí, ¿cuántas?* (yes, how many?), *No, no hay* (no, there isn't any), or *Se nos acabó* (we ran out).

**Asking for it.** You already know *¿Me da…, por favor?* from the taquería. It works at any counter: *¿Me da una botella de agua, por favor?* Containers make orders clear: *una botella de* (a bottle of), *una lata de* (a can of), *un paquete de* (a pack of), *una caja de* (a box of). *Busco…* is “I'm looking for…,” useful when you do not see the thing at all: *Busco un cargador para celular* (a phone charger).

**Finishing and paying.** You will be asked *¿Algo más?* and you answer *No, es todo* (Unit 2). Ask the total with *¿Cuánto es?* (Unit 3). Then the method: *¿Aceptan tarjeta?* “Do you take cards?” A shop may answer *Solo efectivo* (cash only). *¿Me da una bolsa?* asks for a bag; some shops charge for one or do not hand them out, so you may hear *No tenemos bolsas.*

**At the pharmacy.** Describe the problem with *algo para* + the problem: *algo para el dolor de cabeza* (something for a headache), *algo para la tos* (for a cough), *algo para la garganta* (for a sore throat), *algo para el estómago* (for an upset stomach). Two follow-up questions matter: *¿Necesito receta?* “Do I need a prescription?” and *¿Cada cuánto lo tomo?* “How often do I take it?” Ask the pharmacist to write it down if you are not sure: *¿Me lo puede escribir, por favor?*

This unit teaches language, not medical advice. The products in the conversation are generic practice items, not recommendations.

</div>

<p class="scene no-speak">A customer buys water at a corner store, then walks into a pharmacy next door. The prices are invented.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Customer:</span> <span lang="es">Buenas tardes. ¿Tiene agua mineral?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Good afternoon. Do you have mineral water?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Clerk:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, ¿cuántas?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, how many?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Customer:</span> <span lang="es">¿Me da dos botellas, por favor? ¿Y tienen pilas?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Could you give me two bottles, please? And do you have batteries?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Clerk:</span> <span lang="es">No, no hay. ¿Algo más?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">No, there aren't any. Anything else?</span></p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Customer:</span> <span lang="es">Buenas tardes. Busco algo para el dolor de cabeza.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Good afternoon. I'm looking for something for a headache.</span></p>

<div class="note" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Tiene* is TYEH-neh, two syllables; the *ie* glides together. *Hay* sounds like English “eye.” *Botella* is boh-TEH-yah: in Mexico *ll* sounds like the *y* in “yes.” *Efectivo* is eh-fek-TEE-voh. *Receta* is reh-SEH-tah; the *c* before *e* is an *s* sound in Mexico. *Pastillas* is pahs-TEE-yahs. *Cuánto* is KWAHN-toh, one quick *kw* at the start.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Greet, then ask *¿Tiene…?* and *¿Me da…, por favor?* Use *busco…* when you cannot see it. Pay with *¿Cuánto es?* and *¿Aceptan tarjeta?* At the pharmacy: *algo para* + the problem, *¿Necesito receta?*, *¿Cada cuánto lo tomo?*, and *¿Me lo puede escribir?*

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me · shadowing</h3>

<p>Play the model and speak the customer's lines in both shops. Notice that every exchange starts with *buenas tardes* before the request. Say *¿Tiene…?* with a rising tone so it sounds like a question, and keep *¿Aceptan tarjeta?* as one smooth phrase.</p>

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *¿Tiene…?* asks one person (as *usted*); *¿Tienen…?* asks about the shop as a whole.
2. “No, there isn't any.”
3. *¿Me da un paquete de galletas, por favor?*
4. *¿Aceptan tarjeta?* *Solo efectivo* means cash only.
5. *Busco algo para el estómago.*
6. *¿Necesito receta? ¿Cada cuánto lo tomo?*

1. A model: *Buenas tardes. ¿Tiene pilas? ¿Me da dos, por favor? ¿Cuánto es?*
