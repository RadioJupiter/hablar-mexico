# Unit 4 · Directions and transport

When you are on foot in an unfamiliar neighborhood, a short, polite question will usually get you a short, useful answer. This unit is asking where something is, following a direction, and talking about the *camión*.

## Directions and transport {#main}

<p class="meta">About 40 minutes · Situation: asking the way, and checking that a bus goes where you need</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Stop someone with a greeting and *disculpe* (“excuse me”), then ask your question with *usted*. You are speaking to a stranger.

**Where is it?** *¿Dónde queda…?* is the everyday Mexican way to ask where a place is. *¿Dónde está…?* is also correct. *¿Cómo llego a…?* is “How do I get to…?”

**The answer you will hear.** *Siga derecho* is “keep going straight” (*usted*). *Dé vuelta a la derecha* is “turn right”; *dé vuelta a la izquierda* is “turn left.” Distance is often in *cuadras* (blocks): *a dos cuadras*, *a tres cuadras*. *Está a la vuelta* means it is just around the corner. *Frente a* is “across from / facing”; *junto a* is “next to”; *entre* is “between.”

**The bus.** In Mexico the usual word for a city bus is *camión*. The *Diccionario de americanismos* records this Mexican sense: a passenger bus, not only a freight truck. (Mexico also uses *camión* for a freight truck, so the situation tells you which one it is.) *¿Este camión llega a…?* is “Does this bus go to…?” *¿Dónde me bajo?* is “Where do I get off?” *Me bajo en…* is “I get off at….” *El metro* is the subway in cities that have one. This course does not depend on one city's map: the lines work for a bus, a metro station, or a street.

**Going and being.** *Voy a…* is “I'm going to….” *Está* locates a place or a thing: *La estación está a dos cuadras.* *Queda* does the same job in the question *¿Dónde queda?*

**Soon, but not always instantly.** You may hear *Ahorita pasa el camión.* In that sentence *ahorita* means the bus is coming soon, not that it is already in front of you. Take it as “in a moment / shortly” unless the speaker's tone clearly says “right now.” Unit 6 comes back to this word.

The street names in the conversation are invented.

</div>

<h3>Model conversation</h3>

<p class="scene no-speak">On a sidewalk, a visitor asks a stranger for the way to the metro, then checks with a driver whether the bus goes there. Street names are invented.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Disculpe, buenas tardes. ¿Dónde queda el metro?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me, good afternoon. Where is the metro?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Passerby:</span> <span lang="es">Siga derecho dos cuadras y dé vuelta a la izquierda. Está frente al mercado.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Go straight two blocks and turn left. It's across from the market.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Left, across from the market?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Passerby:</span> <span lang="es">Sí. Si no, tome este camión. Ahorita pasa. ¿Va al centro?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes. If not, take this bus. It's coming shortly. Are you going downtown?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, voy al metro. Muchas gracias.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, I'm going to the metro. Thank you very much.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Disculpe, ¿este camión llega al metro?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me, does this bus go to the metro?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Driver:</span> <span lang="es">Sí. Bájese en la segunda parada, junto a la estación.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes. Get off at the second stop, next to the station.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">¿La segunda parada? Perfecto. Gracias.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">The second stop? Perfect. Thank you.</span></p>

<div class="note" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Disculpe* is dees-KOOL-peh. *Queda* is KEH-dah, with a tap *r*-less *d* in the middle that stays light. *Izquierda* is ees-KYER-dah; the *z* is an *s* in Mexico, and *quie* is one syllable: kyehr. *Derecha* is deh-REH-chah, with a single tap *r*. *Cuadra* is KWAH-drah. *Camión* is kah-MYOHN, stress on the last syllable; do not say it as English “camion.” *Parada* is pah-RAH-dah. When you repeat a direction back (*¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado?*), keep it short and clear; that confirmation is part of the conversation, not extra politeness.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Stop a stranger with *disculpe* and *usted*. Ask *¿Dónde queda…?* Follow *siga derecho*, *dé vuelta a la derecha* or *a la izquierda*, and *cuadras*. A city bus is a *camión*. *¿Este camión llega a…?* checks the route. *Ahorita pasa* means it is coming soon.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me · shadowing</h3>

<p>Play the model once for the shape of the exchange: question, directions, then a confirmation repeated back. Play it again and speak the visitor's lines, including the repeated *¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado?* and *¿La segunda parada?* Repeating a direction to check it is a skill, not a hesitation.</p>

### Practice

1. From memory, give directions to a place that is three blocks straight ahead, then right, and across from a church (*una iglesia*).
2. Write two lines: you are going to the metro, and you want to know where to get off.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *Disculpe, ¿dónde queda el metro?* (A greeting such as *buenas tardes* may come first.)
2. *Siga derecho dos cuadras y dé vuelta a la izquierda.*
3. It is just around the corner.
4. A city bus (a passenger bus). It can also mean a freight truck in other contexts, but this is the usual transport sense.
5. *¿Este camión llega a la estación?*
6. No. *Ahorita* here means the bus is coming soon, not that it is already in front of you.

**Practice.**
1. A model: *Siga derecho tres cuadras y dé vuelta a la derecha. Está frente a la iglesia.*
2. A model: *Voy al metro. ¿Dónde me bajo?*
