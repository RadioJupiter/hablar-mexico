# Unit 5 · Meeting people

Once you can greet someone, the next conversation is often a name, a place you are from, and a little about family. This unit stays in that range. It does not ask you to tell your life story.

## Meeting people {#main}

<p class="meta">About 40 minutes · Situation: introductions, where you are from, and family in short form</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A first meeting in Mexico often has three pieces: a name, a *mucho gusto*, and one or two facts. You already have the greeting and *mucho gusto* from Unit 1. Here you add introductions, origin, and close family.

**Introducing someone.** *Te presento a…* is informal (*tú*): “I'd like you to meet….” *Le presento a…* is the *usted* form, the safer one when the person you are speaking to is older or new to you. After the name, *Ella es mi amiga Elena* or *Él es mi esposo, Luis.* *Amigo* and *amiga* are “friend.” *Esposo* and *esposa* are “husband” and “wife.” *Pareja* is “partner,” useful when you need a word that does not specify legal status. *Hijo* and *hija* are “son” and “daughter”; *hijos* can mean “children” as a group. *Papá* and *mamá*, *hermano* and *hermana*, *tío* and *tía* are the close family set.

**Your name, again.** *Me llamo…* is still the basic line. *Soy Elena* also works once the greeting is done. *Soy* comes from *ser*, the verb for who or what something is: *Soy Elena. Somos amigos. Ella es mi hermana.* *Estar* is for how or where someone is: *Estoy bien. Está en casa.* Do not use *estar* for your name or for a family relationship.

**Where you are from.** *¿De dónde eres?* is *tú*. *¿De dónde es?* is *usted*. *Soy de…* plus a country or a city: *Soy de Estados Unidos. Soy de Texas.* *Vivo en…* is “I live in….” If someone asks a follow-up, a short answer is enough.

**Work and family questions.** *¿A qué te dedicas?* (“What do you do?”) is common small talk, but you do not owe a long answer: *Trabajo en…* or *Estoy de visita* (“I'm visiting”) is plenty. Questions about children or a spouse are also common in some conversations. You can answer in one line, or you can decline politely: *Por ahora no, gracias por preguntar* or simply *Así es*, then move on. This unit teaches the questions so you can recognize them, not so you have to ask them.

Use *usted* with a friend's parent until you are invited to do otherwise.

<h3>Model conversation</h3>

<p class="scene no-speak">Elena introduces a visitor to her mother. The visitor uses usted with the mother and tú with Elena.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Elena:</span> <span lang="es">Mamá, te presento a mi amigo Sam. Sam, ella es mi mamá, la señora Teresa.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Mom, I'd like you to meet my friend Sam. Sam, this is my mom, Señora Teresa.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Sam:</span> <span lang="es">Muy buenas tardes. Mucho gusto, señora. Me llamo Sam.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Good afternoon. Pleased to meet you, ma'am. My name is Sam.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Teresa:</span> <span lang="es">El gusto es mío, Sam. ¿De dónde es usted?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">The pleasure is mine, Sam. Where are you from?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Sam:</span> <span lang="es">Soy de Estados Unidos, pero ahora vivo en Texas. ¿Y usted es de aquí?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">I'm from the United States, but I live in Texas now. And are you from here?</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Teresa:</span> <span lang="es">Sí, soy de aquí. ¿Y ya conoce a mis otros hijos? Elena tiene un hermano, Pablo.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Yes, I am. And have you already met my other children? Elena has a brother, Pablo.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Sam:</span> <span lang="es">Todavía no. Mucho gusto en conocerla.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Not yet. It's a pleasure to meet you.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Teresa:</span> <span lang="es">Igualmente. Pase usted, siéntese.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Likewise. Come in, have a seat.</span></p>
<p class="line"><span class="who">Sam:</span> <span lang="es">Con permiso. Gracias.</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Excuse me (as I come in). Thank you.</span></p>

<div class="note" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Presento* is preh-SEN-toh. *Esposo* is ehs-POH-soh; *esposa* is ehs-POH-sah. *Hijo* is EE-hoh, silent *h* and a soft *j*. *Hija* is EE-hah. That *j* is the same back *h* as in *Jamaica* in Unit 2; do not make it an English *h* that disappears, and do not make it a hard English *j*. *Tío* is TEE-oh, two vowels in two syllables. *Teresa* is teh-REH-sah, with a single tap *r*. *Conocerla* is koh-noh-SEHR-lah. *Con permiso* is kohm pehr-MEE-soh; keep both *s* sounds.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

*Te presento a…* is informal; *le presento a…* is *usted*. Use *ser* for who you are and where you are from (*soy de…*), and *estar* for how or where you are (*estoy bien*). *¿De dónde es usted?* is the safer origin question with someone you have just met. *Con permiso* as you come in.

</div>

### Practice

1. From memory, introduce yourself to a friend's father with *usted*: greeting, name, *mucho gusto*, and where you are from.
2. Write one line each for: this is my friend; this is my sister; we are from here.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *Le presento*, because *le* goes with *usted*, the safer form with an older person you have just met. *Te presento* is *tú*.
2. *Soy de Estados Unidos, pero vivo en Texas.* (The *pero* clause is optional if you only need the country.)
3. Because *ser* is the verb for who or what someone is. *Estar* is for how or where someone is, as in *estoy bien*.
4. *¿De dónde es usted?*
5. *Mis hijos* means “my children.” *Mi hija* means “my daughter.”
6. *Con permiso.* You can add *gracias.*

**Practice.**
1. A model: *Buenas tardes, señor. Mucho gusto. Me llamo…. Soy de…*
2. *Él / Ella es mi amigo / amiga. Ella es mi hermana. Somos de aquí.*
