# Unit 2 · At a taquería or café

You can eat well in Mexico with a short set of order lines. This unit is about getting a taco or a drink, saying whether you will eat here, and asking for the bill.

## At a taquería or café {#main}

<p class="meta">About 40 minutes · Situation: ordering food and drink at a casual counter</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

At a taquería or a small café, start with a greeting from Unit 1, then order in a complete chunk. You do not need a long sentence.

**How to order.** *¿Me da…?* (“Could you give me…?”) is a polite, everyday way to ask for food at a counter. *¿Me pone…?* is very common at taco stands: “Could you put me…?” Both are softer than a bare *Quiero…* (“I want…”), though *Quiero…* is understood. Add *por favor*. For more than one item, put the number first: *¿Me da dos de pastor, por favor?*

**The taco itself.** *Una de pastor* is “one pastor taco,” with *taco* often left out because it is obvious. Common fillings include *pastor*, *suadero*, *bistec*, *chorizo*, *carnitas*, *carne asada*, and *pollo*. *Con todo* means “with everything,” usually onion, cilantro, and salsa, according to the stand. If you want less, say *sin cebolla* (no onion), *sin cilantro*, or *con poca salsa*.

**Quesadillas.** In much of Mexico a *quesadilla* has cheese. In parts of Mexico City, people may order a quesadilla and be asked *¿Con queso?* — “with cheese?” That is a real regional usage, not a joke. If you want cheese, say *con queso*.

**Here or to go.** You will hear *¿Para aquí o para llevar?* — “For here or to go?” Answers: *Para aquí* or *Para llevar*.

**Drinks.** *¿Me da un agua de horchata?* or *de jamaica* or *de limón* (fresh aguas). *Una Coca* or *un café* are equally ordinary. *Un agua* keeps the *un* even though *agua* is feminine, because the stressed *a-* at the start of the word is easier to hear that way.

**The bill and a thanks.** *La cuenta, por favor.* If you paid in cash and are waiting, *¿Me da el cambio, por favor?* When the food is good, *Está rico* or *Está muy rico, gracias* is a natural comment, not a formal review.

**Tú or usted.** At a counter, either can appear. *Usted* (*¿Me da…?*, *¿Me puede dar…?*) is never wrong for a beginner adult, and it is the safer default with someone you do not know. Some casual stands will speak to you in *tú*; you can follow their lead.

The prices in the conversation below are invented practice numbers, not real market prices.

<p class="scene no-speak">Midday at a taquería counter. The visitor greets first, then orders. Prices are invented.</p>

<p class="line"><span class="who">Visitor:</span> <span lang="es">Buenas tardes. ¿Me da dos de pastor y una de suadero, por favor?</span> <span class="gloss no-speak">Good afternoon. Could I have two pastor tacos and one suadero, please?</span></p>

<div class="note" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Pronunciation</h3>

*Me da* is two short syllables: meh DAH. *Pastor* is pahs-TOR, with the stress on the last syllable. *Suadero* is swah-DEH-roh. *Cebolla* is seh-BOH-yah; in Mexico *ll* usually sounds like a *y*. *Horchata* is or-CHAH-tah (silent *h*). *Jamaica* is hah-MY-kah, with the Mexican *j* as a breathy *h* from the back of the mouth, not an English *j*. *Cuenta* is KWEN-tah. Keep *rico* as REE-koh, not “rye-co.”

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Greet, then order with *¿Me da…?* or *¿Me pone…?* and *por favor*. *Con todo* is “with everything”; *sin cebolla* leaves the onion off. *Para aquí* is for here; *para llevar* is to go. Ask for the bill with *La cuenta, por favor.* The prices in this unit are invented.

</div>

2. Write the cook's two usual questions after an order (*with everything?* and *for here or to go?*) in Spanish, and your answers.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. *¿Me da dos de suadero, por favor?* (or *¿Me pone dos de suadero, por favor?*).
2. *Esta (or una) con todo, y la otra sin cebolla.* A short version also works: *Una con todo y una sin cebolla.*
3. *Para aquí* means you will eat there; *para llevar* means you are taking the food with you.
4. *La cuenta, por favor.* or *¿Me da la cuenta, por favor?*
5. *Está muy rico, gracias.* (or *Está rico*).
6. Because it is practice material, not a real or current price.

**Practice.**
1. A model: *¿Me da una de pastor con todo y una sin salsa, para llevar, por favor? ¿Me pone un café?*
2. *¿Con todo?* — *Sí, con todo.* (or a *sin…* answer). *¿Para aquí o para llevar?* — *Para llevar.* (or *Para aquí.*)
