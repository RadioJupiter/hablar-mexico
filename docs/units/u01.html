Unit 1 · Greetings and small talk

A first conversation in Mexico starts with a greeting, a name, and a sense of how close you are to the person in front of you. This unit gives you those lines, and the two forms of you.

Greetings and small talk

Greetings and small talk

About 40 minutes · Situation: saying hello, giving your name, and asking someone to repeat

Learn

In Mexico you greet people when you walk into a small shop, step into an elevator with someone already there, or start talking to a stranger. Starting with a request and no greeting can feel abrupt.

Time of day. Hola works at any hour. Buenos días is the morning greeting and often runs into the early afternoon. Buenas tardes follows, through the afternoon. Buenas noches is for the evening, and it is also used when you are leaving at night.

How are you? ¿Cómo estás? uses tú, the informal you. ¿Cómo está? uses usted, the formal you. A steady reply is Bien, gracias, ¿y tú? or, with usted, Bien, gracias, ¿y usted? You can also say Más o menos if you are only so-so. ¿Qué tal? is friendly and widely usable; it is a safe middle step when you want something warmer than a bare hola and less informal than ¿qué onda?

Informal: ¿Qué onda? This is a very common Mexican greeting among friends and peers, along the lines of “what's up?” The Diccionario de americanismos records it in Mexico both as a way to ask what is going on and as a greeting, and marks it as popular speech. Use it with people you already talk to informally. Do not open with it to an older stranger, a formal office, or someone you have just addressed as usted.

Tú and usted. Usted is the safer first choice with people you do not know, with older people, and in more formal service. Tú is usual with friends, many people your own age, and a good deal of casual stand and café talk. If you are unsure, start with usted. Mexicans will often move you to tú themselves. For more than one person, Mexico uses ustedes in every situation. Vosotros is not used here, and this course does not teach it.

When you did not hear. ¿Mande? is the usual polite way, in Mexico, to show you did not catch what someone said, or to answer when someone calls you. The Diccionario del español de México describes it as a courtesy expression for exactly those two cases. ¿Qué? can sound blunt. ¿Cómo? is also understood, but ¿mande? is the form you will be thanked for using.

Names. Me llamo… is “my name is….” After a name, Mucho gusto is “pleased to meet you.” A natural reply is Igualmente or El gusto es mío.

Leaving. Hasta luego and Nos vemos are everyday goodbyes. Adiós is fine too. Que te vaya bien (or Que le vaya bien with usted) is a warm way to send someone off.

Model conversation

Late morning, on the street. Marta and Diego already know each other. Then Marta introduces Diego to Doña Rosa, who is older. The prices and names here are invented.

Marta: Hola, Diego. ¿Qué onda? Hi, Diego. What's up?

Diego: ¿Qué onda, Marta? Bien, ¿y tú? What's up, Marta? Good, and you?

Marta: Bien, gracias. Te presento a la señora Rosa. Good, thanks. I'd like to introduce you to Señora Rosa.

Diego: Buenos días. Mucho gusto, señora. Me llamo Diego. Good morning. Pleased to meet you, ma'am. My name is Diego.

Rosa: ¿Mande? No le oí el nombre. Pardon? I didn't catch the name.

Diego: Diego. Mucho gusto. Diego. Pleased to meet you.

Rosa: El gusto es mío, Diego. ¿Cómo está? The pleasure is mine, Diego. How are you?

Diego: Muy bien, gracias, ¿y usted? Very well, thank you, and you?

Rosa: Bien, gracias. Que les vaya bien. Well, thank you. All the best to you both.

Marta: Igualmente, que le vaya bien. Nos vemos, Diego. And to you. See you, Diego.

Pronunciation

Spanish has five vowel sounds, and they stay put. They do not glide the way many English vowels do:

  • a as in father, short: Marta, gracias
  • e as in the first part of they, short: mesa, usted
  • i as in machine: Diego, días
  • o as in note, but short, without a w off-glide: hola, onda
  • u as in rude: gusto, mucho

H is silent, so hola is OH-lah, not “holla.” In Mexico, c before e or i, and z, sound like s (gracias, días), not like the th of much of Spain. Stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable when a word ends in a vowel, n, or s (HO-la, GRA-cias). A written accent marks a different stress (días, cómo, usted has no accent and is stressed on the last syllable: us-TED).

When you say ¿Qué onda? keep the vowels clean: keh ON-dah. Do not turn onda into “under.”

Remember

Greet first. Use usted when you are unsure, and tú with friends. ¿Qué onda? is informal. ¿Mande? is the polite way to ask someone to repeat. Plural you is ustedes, never vosotros. H is silent: hola is OH-lah.

Watch me · shadowing

Play the model conversation once and only listen. Play it a second time and speak along with every Spanish line, including Rosa's ¿Mande? No le oí el nombre. Keep the vowels short. Notice the shift: Marta and Diego use tú and ¿qué onda? with each other, and both switch to usted with Doña Rosa.

Together · you speak

You meet an acquaintance, then an older neighbor. Say your lines out loud. Do not read the hints first.

An acquaintance greets you informally: ¿Qué onda? ¿Cómo estás?

You:

You then greet Doña Rosa, give your name, and wait. She says ¿Mande?

You:

She says Mucho gusto. ¿Cómo está?

You:

Hint 1: the informal reply

A natural reply is Bien, gracias, ¿y tú? You may also return ¿Qué onda? and then that line.

Hint 2: after ¿Mande?

Repeat your name clearly, and you can add Mucho gusto again: your name, then Mucho gusto.

Hint 3: the formal how-are-you

Muy bien, gracias, ¿y usted? Keep usted with her, even if you just used tú with your friend.

Check

These questions use only what this unit taught. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. It is 10 a.m. and you walk into a small shop. What greeting fits?
  2. You are speaking to an older person you have just met. Do you ask ¿Cómo estás? or ¿Cómo está?, and why?
  3. A friend greets you with ¿Qué onda? Write a natural reply.
  4. You did not catch someone's name. What do you say, and why is ¿Qué? a weaker choice here?
  5. How do you say “pleased to meet you,” and what is one short reply?
  6. When you are talking to two people in Mexico, which plural you do you use?

Practice

  1. Say out loud, without reading, a morning greeting, your name, and mucho gusto.
  2. For each person, choose tú or usted and write the “how are you?” question: (a) a close friend; (b) an older neighbor you are meeting for the first time; (c) two strangers at a shop counter.
  3. Shadow the model conversation two more times, then say Diego's formal lines from memory.
