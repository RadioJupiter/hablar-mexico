Unit 4 · Directions and transport

When you are on foot in an unfamiliar neighborhood, a short, polite question will usually get you a short, useful answer. This unit is asking where something is, following a direction, and talking about the camión.

Directions and transport

Directions and transport

About 40 minutes · Situation: asking the way, and checking that a bus goes where you need

Learn

Stop someone with a greeting and disculpe (“excuse me”), then ask your question with usted. You are speaking to a stranger.

Where is it? ¿Dónde queda…? is the everyday Mexican way to ask where a place is. ¿Dónde está…? is also correct. ¿Cómo llego a…? is “How do I get to…?”

The answer you will hear. Siga derecho is “keep going straight” (usted). Dé vuelta a la derecha is “turn right”; dé vuelta a la izquierda is “turn left.” Distance is often in cuadras (blocks): a dos cuadras, a tres cuadras. Está a la vuelta means it is just around the corner. Frente a is “across from / facing”; junto a is “next to”; entre is “between.”

The bus. In Mexico the usual word for a city bus is camión. The Diccionario de americanismos records this Mexican sense: a passenger bus, not only a freight truck. (Mexico also uses camión for a freight truck, so the situation tells you which one it is.) ¿Este camión llega a…? is “Does this bus go to…?” ¿Dónde me bajo? is “Where do I get off?” Me bajo en… is “I get off at….” El metro is the subway in cities that have one. This course does not depend on one city's map: the lines work for a bus, a metro station, or a street.

Going and being. Voy a… is “I'm going to….” Está locates a place or a thing: La estación está a dos cuadras. Queda does the same job in the question ¿Dónde queda?

Soon, but not always instantly. You may hear Ahorita pasa el camión. In that sentence ahorita means the bus is coming soon, not that it is already in front of you. Take it as “in a moment / shortly” unless the speaker's tone clearly says “right now.” Unit 6 comes back to this word.

The street names in the conversation are invented.

Model conversation

On a sidewalk, a visitor asks a stranger for the way to the metro, then checks with a driver whether the bus goes there. Street names are invented.

Visitor: Disculpe, buenas tardes. ¿Dónde queda el metro? Excuse me, good afternoon. Where is the metro?

Passerby: Siga derecho dos cuadras y dé vuelta a la izquierda. Está frente al mercado. Go straight two blocks and turn left. It's across from the market.

Visitor: ¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado? Left, across from the market?

Passerby: Sí. Si no, tome este camión. Ahorita pasa. ¿Va al centro? Yes. If not, take this bus. It's coming shortly. Are you going downtown?

Visitor: Sí, voy al metro. Muchas gracias. Yes, I'm going to the metro. Thank you very much.

Visitor: Disculpe, ¿este camión llega al metro? Excuse me, does this bus go to the metro?

Driver: Sí. Bájese en la segunda parada, junto a la estación. Yes. Get off at the second stop, next to the station.

Visitor: ¿La segunda parada? Perfecto. Gracias. The second stop? Perfect. Thank you.

Pronunciation

Disculpe is dees-KOOL-peh. Queda is KEH-dah, with a tap r-less d in the middle that stays light. Izquierda is ees-KYER-dah; the z is an s in Mexico, and quie is one syllable: kyehr. Derecha is deh-REH-chah, with a single tap r. Cuadra is KWAH-drah. Camión is kah-MYOHN, stress on the last syllable; do not say it as English “camion.” Parada is pah-RAH-dah. When you repeat a direction back (¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado?), keep it short and clear; that confirmation is part of the conversation, not extra politeness.

Remember

Stop a stranger with disculpe and usted. Ask ¿Dónde queda…? Follow siga derecho, dé vuelta a la derecha or a la izquierda, and cuadras. A city bus is a camión. ¿Este camión llega a…? checks the route. Ahorita pasa means it is coming soon.

Watch me · shadowing

Play the model once for the shape of the exchange: question, directions, then a confirmation repeated back. Play it again and speak the visitor's lines, including the repeated ¿A la izquierda, frente al mercado? and ¿La segunda parada? Repeating a direction to check it is a skill, not a hesitation.

Together · you speak

You are lost and then you are boarding a bus. Say your lines out loud.

Stop a stranger and ask where the market is.

You:

The person says: Siga derecho una cuadra y dé vuelta a la derecha. Está a la vuelta. Confirm that.

You:

At the curb, ask the driver whether this bus goes to the market, and where you should get off.

You:

Hint 1: stopping someone

Disculpe, buenas tardes. ¿Dónde queda el mercado?

Hint 2: confirming

¿A la derecha, a la vuelta? or ¿Una cuadra, a la derecha, y está a la vuelta?

Hint 3: the bus

Disculpe, ¿este camión llega al mercado? ¿Dónde me bajo?

Check

These questions use only what this unit taught. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Write a polite question asking a stranger where the metro is.
  2. How do you say “go straight two blocks and turn left,” using usted?
  3. What does está a la vuelta mean?
  4. In Mexico, what does camión usually mean when you are talking about getting across town?
  5. Write the line you use to ask whether this bus goes to the station.
  6. Someone says Ahorita pasa el camión. Does that guarantee the bus is already there? Why or why not?

Practice

  1. From memory, give directions to a place that is three blocks straight ahead, then right, and across from a church (una iglesia).
  2. Write two lines: you are going to the metro, and you want to know where to get off.
