Unit 9 · When something goes wrong

Sooner or later you will not understand, you will lose something, or you will feel unwell. This unit gives you calm, simple lines for getting help, so a problem does not leave you without words.

When something goes wrong

When something goes wrong

About 45 minutes · Situation: not understanding, asking for help, and explaining a problem

Learn

When you do not understand. ¿Mande? (Unit 1) asks someone to repeat. When repeating is not enough, use these. No entiendo is “I don't understand.” ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? is “Could you speak more slowly, please?” (usted). ¿Qué quiere decir…? is “What does … mean?”: ¿Qué quiere decir “camión”? ¿Cómo se dice … en español? is “How do you say … in Spanish?” Saying Estoy aprendiendo español (I'm learning Spanish) usually makes people slow down and help.

Asking for help. ¿Me puede ayudar? is “Can you help me?” to someone you address as usted, which is right with a stranger. Start with Disculpe (Unit 4) to get their attention: Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar?

Explaining a problem. Simple past forms of a few verbs go a long way. Perdí mi celular is “I lost my phone.” Perdí mi cartera is “I lost my wallet.” Me robaron la cartera is “My wallet was stolen” (literally, “they stole the wallet from me”). Me equivoqué de camión is “I took the wrong bus.” Estoy perdido (a man) or Estoy perdida (a woman) is “I'm lost.” Learn these as whole lines; the past tense itself comes in a later unit.

Feeling unwell. Me siento mal is “I feel sick.” Pain uses doler, which works like gustar in Unit 8: the thing that hurts is the subject. Me duele la cabeza (my head hurts), Me duele el estómago (my stomach hurts), Me duelen los pies (my feet hurt, plural). Spanish uses la or el here, not “my.” Necesito un médico or Necesito un doctor is “I need a doctor.”

Emergencies. ¡Ayuda! is “Help!” Llame a una ambulancia is “Call an ambulance” (usted). Es una emergencia is “It's an emergency.” Mexico's national emergency number is 911.

The situations in the conversation are invented practice. This unit teaches language, not medical or legal advice.

Model conversation

A visitor who has lost a phone asks a stranger, then a police officer, for help. Later, at a hotel desk, the visitor feels unwell. Everything is invented.

Visitor: Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar? Perdí mi celular. Excuse me, can you help me? I lost my phone.

Woman: Ay, qué mal. Pregúntele al policía de la esquina. Oh, that's bad. Ask the police officer on the corner.

Visitor: Perdón, no entiendo. ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? Estoy aprendiendo español. Sorry, I don't understand. Could you speak more slowly, please? I'm learning Spanish.

Woman: Claro. Ahí, en la esquina, hay un policía. Of course. There, on the corner, there's a police officer.

Visitor: Muchas gracias. Thank you very much.

Later, at the hotel desk.

Visitor: Buenas noches. Me siento mal. Me duele mucho el estómago. Good evening. I feel sick. My stomach hurts a lot.

Clerk: ¿Necesita un médico? Do you need a doctor?

Visitor: Sí, por favor. ¿Me puede ayudar a llamar? Yes, please. Can you help me call?

Clerk: Sí, cómo no. Ahorita le llamo. Yes, of course. I'll call for you right away.

Pronunciation

Entiendo is en-TYEN-doh. Despacio is des-PAH-syoh; the ci is an s sound in Mexico. Ayudar is ah-yoo-DAR, with a tapped r at the end. Perdí is per-DEE, stress on the last syllable because of the accent; perdido is per-DEE-doh. Duele is DWEH-leh, two syllables. Emergencia is eh-mer-HEN-syah: the g before e is the breathy h. Ambulancia is ahm-boo-LAHN-syah.

Remember

Say no entiendo and ¿puede hablar más despacio? early. Get help with disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar? Explain with whole lines: perdí mi…, me robaron…, estoy perdido / perdida. Pain: me duele + one thing, me duelen + several. Emergencies: ¡ayuda!, llame a una ambulancia; the number in Mexico is 911.

Watch me · shadowing

Play the model and speak the visitor's lines. Say no entiendo and más despacio calmly and clearly; these are the lines you most need when you are flustered. Pay attention to me duele mucho el estómago: mucho goes after the verb.

Together · you speak

Three small problems. Say your lines out loud.

Someone gives you directions very fast and you did not follow.

You:

You have lost your wallet and you approach a stranger.

You:

At a hotel desk, you have a bad headache and want a doctor.

You:

Hint 1: too fast

Perdón, no entiendo. ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? Estoy aprendiendo español.

Hint 2: a lost wallet

Disculpe, ¿me puede ayudar? Perdí mi cartera.

Hint 3: feeling unwell

Buenas noches. Me siento mal. Me duele mucho la cabeza. Necesito un médico.

Check

These questions use only what this unit taught. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Ask someone (usted) to speak more slowly.
  2. How do you ask what a word means, and how to say a word in Spanish?
  3. Get a stranger's attention and ask for help.
  4. Say that you lost your phone, and that your wallet was stolen.
  5. Why is it me duele la cabeza but me duelen los pies?
  6. What is Mexico's national emergency number, and how do you say “Call an ambulance”?

Practice

  1. Write what you would say if you got on the wrong bus and are now lost.
  2. Write three pain sentences: one with duele, one with duelen, and one with mucho.
